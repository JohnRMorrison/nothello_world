Probing Results Summary
=======================
Date: 2026-03-07
Probe: Nanda-style 3-mode linear probe (even/odd/all)
  Shape: (3, d_model, 8, 8, 3), AdamW lr=1e-4, 2 epochs, batch_size=100
  Positions: 5-54 (skip early/late moves)
  ~100K games, 80/20 train/eval split

If decodability is high, it may reflect input artifacts rather than learned

Chance level: 33.3% (3-class: empty/white/black)

Model descriptions:
  Othello-GPT:         GPT trained to predict next moves in Othello games.
                       This is the standard model from Li et al. / Nanda et al.
  V-table State Pred:  GPT trained to predict an arbitrary 60-dim binary state
                       defined by a random "V-table" that maps each token to a
                       bitmask of dimensions to flip. Not related to Othello rules.
  Bracket Flipper:     GPT trained to predict states in a bracket-flipping game
                       where tokens flip the colors of earlier tokens that share
                       the same group (defined by a random group table).
  Chain Bracket:       Same as Bracket Flipper but with cascading flips — each
                       flipped token can trigger further bracket flips, up to 5
                       rounds of cascading.
  Random Init:         Untrained GPT with random weights. Serves as a baseline
                       for what is decodable purely from input embeddings
                       (token identity + positional encoding).

Model checkpoints:
  Othello-GPT:         ckpts/gpt_synthetic.ckpt
  V-table State Pred:  experiments/.../ckpts/state_pred_vseed42_8L_512d
  Bracket Flipper:     experiments/.../ckpts/bracket_pred_gseed42_8L_512d
  Chain Bracket:       experiments/.../ckpts/chain_bracket_pred_gseed42_8L_512d
  Random Init:         experiments/.../ckpts/random_init_8L_512d

Key observations:
  - Othello-GPT: Progressive buildup 87% -> 99%, peaks at L5. Clear world model.
  - V-table State Pred: Flat ~76.3-76.8%. Only ~3% above random init baseline.
  - Bracket / Chain Bracket: Flat ~58-60%. BELOW random init — learned
    representations actively interfere with Othello decoding.
  - Random Init: Flat ~73%. Sets the "input embedding only" baseline.
  - All non-Othello models: flat or decreasing across layers (no progressive buildup).

PART 2: Probing Othello-GPT for VARIANT board states
================================================================================

Othello-GPT probed for board states under different game rules.
Tests whether variant board states are also decodable from Othello-GPT
activations (which would suggest decodability alone is not evidence of

  1   |  93.86%  |  88.47%  |  80.05%  |  77.94%  |  70.66%  | 71.42%
  2   |  97.05%  |  88.89%  |  80.45%  |  78.43%  |  71.73%  | 71.31%
  3   |  98.39%  |  89.08%  |  80.67%  |  78.92%  |  72.23%  | 71.32%
  4   |  99.08%  |  89.03%  |  80.53%  |  79.11%  |  72.37%  | 71.38%
  5   |  99.20%  |  88.74%  |  80.01%  |  78.71%  |  71.91%  | 71.12%
  6   |  98.54%  |  87.65%  |  78.26%  |  77.04%  |  70.54%  | 70.20%
  7   |  97.97%  |  87.01%  |  77.43%  |  76.38%  |  69.68%  | 70.09%
  8   |  97.95%  |  87.01%  |  77.42%  |  76.38%  |  69.68%  | 70.09%

Chance levels: 33.3% for board variants, 50.0% for V_table (60-dim binary)

Variant descriptions:
  Normal:    Standard Othello (baseline/control)
  No Flip:   Othello but pieces never flip after placement
  Row-Only:  Othello but flips only along rows (not cols/diags)
  Gravity:   Pieces fall to bottom of their column (Connect Four style)
  Shuffled:  Real Othello board state shifted in time by offset=7
  V_table:   Arbitrary flip game (60-dim binary state, not 8x8 board)

Key observations:
  - Normal: +12% progressive buildup (87 -> 99%), peaks at L5.
  - No Flip: High at L0 (87%) then plateaus ~89%. Most decodability is from
    input, only ~2% added by the network.
  - Row-Only, Gravity: Similar pattern — modest L0->L3 increase, then decline.
    Late layers return to ~L0 levels.
  - Shuffled: Small increase L0->L4, then decline. Partial time correlation.
  - V_table: Completely flat ~70-71%. No layer dependence whatsoever.
  - All variants peak at L3-L4 and decline in later layers, suggesting the
    model's later layers specialize for real Othello and reshape representations
    away from variant states.
  - Only real Othello board state shows the characteristic progressive buildup
    through network depth that would indicate a genuine world model.

PART 3: Standard Probe (No Even/Odd Split)
================================================================================

Nanda's 3-mode probe uses separate weight matrices for even-turn and odd-turn
positions, effectively giving the probe access to turn parity. This experiment
tests a standard linear probe nn.Linear(512, 64*3) without that split.

Layer | Othello-GPT | Random Init
------|-------------|------------
  0   |    75.85%   |   74.50%
  1   |    75.92%   |   74.60%
  2   |    75.96%   |   74.51%
  3   |    75.89%   |   74.47%
  4   |    75.75%   |   74.46%
  5   |    75.73%   |   74.40%
  6   |    75.35%   |   74.25%
  7   |    75.37%   |   74.25%
  8   |    75.27%   |   74.22%

Key observations:
  - Without even/odd split, Othello-GPT only reaches ~76% — vs 99% with Nanda's
    probe. The even/odd split accounts for ~23% of accuracy.
  - Othello-GPT is only ~1.2% above random init. Both are flat across layers.
  - This suggests Nanda's high accuracy relies heavily on the probe's built-in
    knowledge of turn parity, not just on the model's representations.

PART 4: Raw Embeddings (resid_pre) vs First Block (resid_post Layer 0)
================================================================================

Tests what can be decoded from token + position embeddings alone (before any
attention or MLP), vs after the first transformer block.

                  | Standard Probe Accuracy
------------------|------------------------
  resid_pre       |       61.37%
  resid_post L0   |       75.82%
  Difference      |      +14.45%

Key observations:
  - resid_pre sees only its own token identity and position. No cross-position
    information (no attention has occurred).
  - The first attention layer adds +14.5% by gathering information from other
    positions in the sequence.
  - 61% from embeddings alone is well above chance (33%), showing that token
    identity and position encoding already carry significant board information.

PART 5: Alternative Board States from Othello-GPT Layer 0
================================================================================

Standard probe on Othello-GPT layer 0 activations, decoding 7 different board
state definitions. Tests whether layer 0 generically supports decoding many
board-state functions (which would undermine the claim that decodability implies
a world model).

Board State        | Standard Probe Accuracy
-------------------|------------------------
  Real Othello     |       75.81%
  No Flip          |       89.17%
  Column-shifted   |       75.88%
  Row-shifted      |       75.85%
  Transposed       |       75.82%
  Color-inverted   |       75.83%
  Cell-permuted    |       75.84%

Variant descriptions:
  Real Othello:    Standard Othello rules (control)
  No Flip:         Pieces placed but never flip — just record where each player
                   moved. ~80.6% agreement with real Othello board state.
  Column-shifted:  Real Othello board with columns shifted by +1 (mod 8)
  Row-shifted:     Real Othello board with rows shifted by +1 (mod 8)
  Transposed:      Real Othello board with rows and columns swapped
  Color-inverted:  Real Othello board with black/white swapped
  Cell-permuted:   Real Othello board with a fixed random permutation of cells

Key observations:
  - All geometric transforms decode at ~75.8% — identical to real Othello.
    A standard probe at layer 0 cannot distinguish the real board from shifted,
    transposed, or permuted versions.
  - No Flip is much easier (89.2%) because it requires no flip computation —
    it only needs to track where each player moved.
  - This confirms that layer 0 representations are not specialized for any
    particular board geometry.

PART 7: Heuristic Features → Board State
================================================================================

Tests whether decision-tree rules (from Teo's interpretability work) can decode
board state using only move-sequence information, with no access to model
activations at all.

Rules come from `rules_060_2000_2-6.json` (8139 rules total, layers 2-6).
Original features reference board state (e.g., A5_mine, B3_theirs, F6_empty,
D3_flipped). We convert them to move-only approximations.

Two filtering strategies:
  Convert: Drop rules with `_flipped` features. Convert `_mine` to "played on
           same-parity turn" and `_theirs` to "played on opposite-parity turn".
           Keeps `_empty` and `_just_played` directly. 4766 surviving rules.
  Strict:  Drop rules with `_flipped`, `_mine`, or `_theirs` features. Only
           uses `_empty` and `_just_played` — directly computable from moves.
           405 surviving rules.

Standard probe (no even/odd split):

                         | Convert (4766 rules) | Strict (405 rules)
-------------------------|----------------------|--------------------
  Heuristic only         |       76.0%          |      72.7%
  Heuristic + Random L0  |       76.2%          |      73.9%
  Random L0 only         |       73.7%          |      73.6%
  Othello-GPT L0 only    |       75.0%          |      75.0%
  Heuristic + Othello L0 |       76.2%          |      74.9%

Nanda even/odd probe:

                              | Convert (4766 rules) | Strict (405 rules)
------------------------------|----------------------|--------------------
  Nanda heuristic only        |       86.4%          |      75.8%
  Nanda heuristic + Othello L0|       89.7%          |      90.7%

For reference, Nanda's probe on Othello-GPT achieves 87% at L0 and 99% at L5.

Key observations:
  - Standard probe: Heuristic features (no activations) match Othello-GPT
    activations (~76% vs ~76%). Adding Othello-GPT L0 provides negligible gain.
  - Nanda even/odd probe on convert heuristics: 86.4% — a large jump from
    76% standard, showing the even/odd split unlocks parity-encoded features.
    But still well below Othello-GPT's 99%.
  - Nanda even/odd + Othello-GPT L0: ~90%. The model adds ~3-4% beyond
    heuristics alone, and ~15% beyond its own standard probe (75% -> 90%).
  - Strict mode is revealing: heuristics alone with Nanda probe = only 75.8%
    (empty/just_played don't benefit from even/odd). But adding Othello-GPT L0
    jumps to 90.7% — the model's representations contain information that
    simple heuristics don't capture, but only accessible with the right probe.
  - The 99% vs 90% gap: Othello-GPT's deeper layers (L3-L5) build
    representations that go well beyond what's available at L0 + heuristics.
    This ~9% gap represents genuine board-state computation by the network.

================================================================================
PART 8: Feature Search — How much board state is recoverable from move history?
================================================================================

Tests whether board state (including flips) can be decoded from move-sequence
features alone, using increasingly powerful probes. No model activations used.

Move-history features (180-d base):
  played[i] (60): 1 if position i has been played by this step
  when[i]   (60): normalized step when position i was played (0 if not played)
  even[i]   (60): 1 if position i was played on an even-numbered turn

Pairwise features (3780-d): base 180-d + played[i]*played[j] for i<j (1770) +
  played[i]*even[i] (60) + even[i]*even[j] for i<j (1770)

--- A. Linear Probes on Move-History Features (80K games) ---

                              | Nanda (even/odd) | Standard
------------------------------|------------------|----------
  Base 180-d                  |     87.3%        |  87.3%
  Pairwise 3780-d             |     87.8%        |    —

Previous results (2K games):
  Base 180-d: 83.4% (Nanda), 83.3% (standard)
  Pairwise 3780-d: 85.5% (Nanda), 85.3% (standard)

Key: scaling from 2K to 80K games improved linear probes by ~4%. Pairwise
features add only ~0.5% at scale (vs 2.1% at 2K games).

--- B. MLP on Move-History Features ---

2-layer MLP: Linear(input_dim, H) -> ReLU -> Linear(H, 64*3)
with Nanda's even/odd split (separate MLPs for even and odd positions).

180-d base features:

  Hidden dim  |  2K games  |  80K games  | 250K games
  ------------|------------|-------------|------------
  Linear      |   83.4%    |   87.3%     |     —
  H = 256     |   86.3%    |   90.4%     |     —
  H = 512     |   86.4%    |   91.9%     |     —
  H = 1024    |   86.6%    |   92.5%     |   93.1%
  H = 2048    |     —      |     —       |   93.3%

3780-d pairwise features (80K games):

  Hidden dim  |  Accuracy
  ------------|----------
  H = 256     |   89.5%
  H = 512     |   89.9%
  H = 1024    |   90.0%

Key observations:
  - MLP on 180-d decisively beats MLP on 3780-d (92.5% vs 90.0% at H=1024).
    The hand-crafted pairwise features hurt — the MLP learns better nonlinear
    combinations from the compact 180-d input.
  - Accuracy scales strongly with both data and model size. 2K -> 80K -> 250K
    games: 86.6% -> 92.5% -> 93.1% for H=1024.
  - H=2048 gives a small bump over H=1024 (+0.2% at 250K games).
  - Best result: 93.3% (H=2048, 250K games) from move-sequence features alone.
    For reference, Nanda's probe on Othello-GPT L0 gets 87.1%.

--- C. Learned Heuristic Combos (80K games) ---

Architecture: heuristic features (4766-d) -> Linear(4766, K) -> ReLU ->
optionally concat with original -> Nanda even/odd probe -> board state.
Uses the 4766 "convert" mode heuristic rules from Part 7.

  Config                    | 2K games | 80K games
  --------------------------|----------|----------
  K=200 concat              |  87.4%   |  90.2%
  K=200 learned only        |  87.7%   |  89.8%
  K=500 concat              |  87.5%   |  90.4%
  K=500 learned only        |  87.6%   |  90.4%
  K=1000 concat             |  87.5%   |  90.3%
  K=1000 learned only       |  87.5%   |  90.4%

Key: peaks at ~90.4% regardless of K — well below MLP on 180-d (92.5%).
The heuristic rules don't provide useful structure beyond what the MLP
learns from raw move features.

--- Summary ---

Best accuracy from move-sequence features alone (no model activations):
  Linear probe:        87.8%  (3780-d pairwise, 80K games)
  Learned heuristic:   90.4%  (K=500, 80K games)
  MLP 180-d:           93.3%  (H=2048, 250K games)  <-- best

For comparison:
  Nanda probe on Othello-GPT L0:  87.1%
  Nanda probe on Othello-GPT L5:  99.2%

A 2-layer MLP on simple move statistics (played/when/parity) surpasses
Othello-GPT L0 by 6.2% and closes much of the gap to L5. The remaining
~6% gap (93.3% -> 99.2%) represents what the deeper transformer layers
compute beyond what is available from move-sequence statistics.
