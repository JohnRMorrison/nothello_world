Variant descriptions:
  Each variant modifies one aspect of standard Othello rules. Games are generated
  by generate_variant_games.py. 2M games per variant.

  Legality-changing variants (different legal move set from standard Othello):
    no_diagonal_flips  — Diagonal directions don't produce flips. Only horizontal
                         and vertical flanking counts. Rule div = 0.540.
    no_row_flips       — Horizontal (row) flips disabled. Only vertical and
                         diagonal flanking counts. Rule div = 0.315.
    no_same_quadrant   — A move cannot flip pieces in the same board quadrant as
                         the placed piece. Rule div = 0.289.
    locked_flips       — Pieces flipped on the previous turn cannot be re-flipped.
                         They are "locked" for one turn. Rule div = 0.335.
    max_three_flips    — A move can flip at most 3 pieces. If no ≤3-flip moves
                         exist, the player forfeits. Rule div = 0.172.
    skip_empty_flips   — Empty squares between flanking pieces don't block flips —
                         the flip "skips" gaps. Rule div = 0.419.
    capture_any        — Legal if adjacent to any opponent piece (no flanking
                         required). Normal flips still apply. Rule div = 0.389.
    adjacent_legal     — Legal if adjacent to ANY occupied square (own or
                         opponent). Normal flips still apply. Rule div = TBD.

  Flip-mechanic variants (same legality rule, different board-state evolution):
    self_flanking      — Own pieces bracketed between new piece and opponent piece
                         flip to opponent's color. ~42 self-flank flips per game.
                         Rule div ≈ 0, trajectory div = 0.57.
    delayed_flips      — Flips are "pending" and applied at the start of the NEXT
                         player's move. Players see a stale board.
                         Rule div ≈ 0, trajectory div = 0.72.

  Control:
    swapped_colors     — Starting pieces have B/W swapped. Rules identical.
                         Tests whether difficulty is rule complexity vs representation.

  Dropped:
    wrap_flips         — Flips wrap around board edges. div = 0.149. Too similar.

Comparison metrics:
  Rule divergence (measure_legal_divergence.py):
    For the same board state, compare legal move sets under standard vs variant
    rules. Jaccard distance: 1 - |intersection| / |union|. LIMITATION: reads 0
    for variants that change flip mechanics without changing legality.

  Trajectory divergence (measure_trajectory_divergence.py):
    Play the SAME moves on both standard and variant boards. Compare legal move
    sets at each turn. Captures divergence from different flip mechanics that
    accumulate over turns. Critical complement to rule divergence.

  Game statistics (compute_game_statistics.py):
    - avg_branching_factor: mean legal moves per position (determines random
      baseline accuracy = 1/BF)
    - avg_game_length: mean moves per game
    - move_entropy: entropy of move frequency distribution (bits)
    - random_top1_acc: expected accuracy of random predictor

  Embedding-based divergence (TODO):
    Run variant games through pretrained OthelloGPT, collect residual stream
    activations at layer 4 or 5, compare distributions via MMD or Fréchet
    distance. Directly measures "how different do these games look to the model."


================================================================================
B. CAUSAL INTERVENTION EXPERIMENTS
================================================================================

Methodology (current, as of v5):
  - Intervene at resid_post of block N using layer N's NATIVE probe for both
    direction computation and local calibration
  - Per-cell binary search for minimal scale that flips probe at target layer
  - Calibration depth: 0 (local), 1 (+1 downstream), 2 (+2 downstream)
  - Measure probe accuracy at all downstream layers (each using its own probe)
  - Measure logit-level metrics: boundary margin, frac_positive, Li et al.
    top-N accuracy, legal probability mass
  - Per-move binary metrics: newly-legal enters top-N, above min-legal,
    newly-illegal dropped from top-N, below min-legal

Previous versions used layer 6 probe directions at layer 5 (cross-layer
mismatch). See abandoned_experiments.txt for v1-v3 details.

Per-move binary metrics (200 games with saved probs):
  Jobs: 5752339 (cd0), 5752340 (cd1), 5752337 (cd2), 5752341 (L4 cd2)

  Metric definitions:
    In Top-N:        newly-legal move appears in model's top-N predictions
                     (N = number of counterfactual-legal moves)
    Above Min-Legal: newly-legal move's prob >= weakest still-legal move's prob
    Dropped Top-N:   newly-illegal move falls out of model's top-N
    Below Min-Legal: newly-illegal move's prob drops below weakest still-legal

  Aggregate results (all conditions, N=1 flips/adds/mixed):

  Setting   | NL In Top-N | NL Above Min-Legal | NI Dropped Top-N | NI Below Min-Legal
  ----------|-------------|---------------------|------------------|-----------
  L5 cd0    |    51%      |         0%          |       31%        |     46%
  L5 cd1    |    69%      |         1%          |       64%        |     86%
  L5 cd2    |    82%      |         5%          |       84%        |     94%
  L4 cd2    |    81%      |         7%          |       79%        |     91%

  Fall-off with N (L5 cd2):
    Newly-legal  In Top-N:        82% (N=1) → 70% (N=2) → 69% (N=3)
    Newly-legal  Above Min-Legal:  5% (N=1) →  8% (N=2) → 12% (N=3)
    Newly-illegal Dropped Top-N:  84% (N=1) → 75% (N=2) → 69% (N=3)
    Newly-illegal Below Min-Legal: 94% (N=1) → 91% (N=2) → 89% (N=3)

  Key findings:
  1. Strong asymmetry: 94% of newly-illegal moves lose "legal" probability
     status, but only 5% of newly-legal moves gain it. Suppression >> promotion.
  2. "In Top-N" is misleadingly high because illegal moves have near-zero
     probability (~0.01%), so a tiny boost suffices to outrank them. "Above
     Min-Legal" is the honest test — newly-legal moves reach ~1-3% probability
     but legal moves sit at ~8-16%.
  3. For add conditions, 100% of newly-illegal moves are on the added cell
     (trivial: occupied square). For flip conditions, 0% are on modified cells
     (genuine flanking logic update).
  4. L4 cd2 ≈ L5 cd2: intervening one layer earlier gives similar results.
     The real lever is calibration depth, not intervention layer.
  5. Calibration depth is the dominant factor: cd0→cd2 at L5 is a much larger
     improvement than L5→L4 at cd2.

experiments/multi_intervention_probs{,_cd0,_cd1}/

B1c. Cascade intervention (Li et al. approach)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Intervene at every layer from start_layer to 7, each using that layer's
native probe for direction and local per-cell scale calibration. This follows
Li et al.'s approach of reinforcing the intervention at each layer rather than
relying on a single-layer intervention to propagate through the network.

  - N = 1, 2, 3, 8 simultaneous interventions
  - 5 conditions: flip_noninteracting, flip_interacting,
    add_noninteracting, add_interacting, mixed
  - Two runs: start at L4 (layers 4-7) and start at L5 (layers 5-7)
  - Saves full probability vectors (--save-probs)

Key findings:
  1. Cascade performs WORSE than single-layer cd2 on all per-move metrics,
     and is roughly comparable to single-layer cd0. The cascade applies
     locally-calibrated interventions at each layer, but the aggregate
     effect is much weaker than a single intervention calibrated to
     propagate 2 layers downstream.
  2. Likely explanation: each transformer block partially undoes the previous
     layer's intervention before the next cascade step is applied. The
     interventions don't stack constructively.
  3. NL Above Min-Legal remains at 0% — newly-legal moves never reach
     the probability floor of existing legal moves under cascade.
  4. Cascade does show slightly lower crosstalk (0.084 vs 0.107 for cd0),
     suggesting the per-layer local calibration is gentler but less effective.

1 (+1 layer downstream), 2 (+2 layers downstream).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A3. Spatially impossible board experiments (GER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cross-wire Othello flanking patterns to create rule sets that are structured
and compressible but cannot be embedded in any consistent 8x8 grid.

Method: For each pattern, with probability beta, swap its ray body (opponents +
terminal) with another pattern of the same direction and length whose target is
in the opposite board half (rows 0-3 vs 4-7). Swaps at the individual pattern
level create intra-cell contradictions: cell 16's right-length-1 pattern still
points to neighbor 17, but its right-length-2 pattern now points to cells from
row 5. No grid can satisfy both.

Spatial properties violated by cross-wiring:
  - Embeddability: no consistent 8x8 grid satisfies all constraints
  - Reflexivity: A→C flanks B, but C→A may not flank B
  - Transitivity: "right from A" → B, "right from B" → C, but C ≠ A+2
  - Collinearity: length-2 and length-3 patterns from same cell disagree on ray
  - Commutativity: right-then-down ≠ down-then-right
  - Distance symmetry: A references B at distance 2, B may never reference A
  - Neighborhood consistency: two directions from A may point to same cell
  - Betweenness: intermediate cells in long patterns absent from shorter patterns

Measured by Grid Embedding Residual (GER): extract all consecutive-pair
constraints from patterns with direction labels, solve least-squares for (x,y)
coordinates of each cell. GER = mean squared residual. Standard Othello → 0.

GER range achievable via cross-wiring: 0.0 to ~0.51. Compare: alpha corruption
has no spatial structure at all but is less compressible.
