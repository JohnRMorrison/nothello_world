Variant descriptions:
  Each variant modifies one aspect of standard Othello rules. Games are generated
  by generate_variant_games.py. 2M games per variant.

  Legality-changing variants (different legal move set from standard Othello):
    no_diagonal_flips  — Diagonal directions don't produce flips. Only horizontal
                         and vertical flanking counts. Rule div = 0.540.
    no_row_flips       — Horizontal (row) flips disabled. Only vertical and
                         diagonal flanking counts. Rule div = 0.315.
    no_same_quadrant   — A move cannot flip pieces in the same board quadrant as
                         the placed piece. Rule div = 0.289.
    locked_flips       — Pieces flipped on the previous turn cannot be re-flipped.
                         They are "locked" for one turn. Rule div = 0.335.
    max_three_flips    — A move can flip at most 3 pieces. If no ≤3-flip moves
                         exist, the player forfeits. Rule div = 0.172.
    skip_empty_flips   — Empty squares between flanking pieces don't block flips —
                         the flip "skips" gaps. Rule div = 0.419.
    capture_any        — Legal if adjacent to any opponent piece (no flanking
                         required). Normal flips still apply. Rule div = 0.389.
    adjacent_legal     — Legal if adjacent to ANY occupied square (own or
                         opponent). Normal flips still apply. Rule div = TBD.

  Flip-mechanic variants (same legality rule, different board-state evolution):
    self_flanking      — Own pieces bracketed between new piece and opponent piece
                         flip to opponent's color. ~42 self-flank flips per game.
                         Rule div ≈ 0, trajectory div = 0.57.
    delayed_flips      — Flips are "pending" and applied at the start of the NEXT
                         player's move. Players see a stale board.
                         Rule div ≈ 0, trajectory div = 0.72.

  Control:
    swapped_colors     — Starting pieces have B/W swapped. Rules identical.
                         Tests whether difficulty is rule complexity vs representation.

  Dropped:
    wrap_flips         — Flips wrap around board edges. div = 0.149. Too similar.

Comparison metrics:
  Rule divergence (measure_legal_divergence.py):
    For the same board state, compare legal move sets under standard vs variant
    rules. Jaccard distance: 1 - |intersection| / |union|. LIMITATION: reads 0
    for variants that change flip mechanics without changing legality.

  Trajectory divergence (measure_trajectory_divergence.py):
    Play the SAME moves on both standard and variant boards. Compare legal move
    sets at each turn. Captures divergence from different flip mechanics that
    accumulate over turns. Critical complement to rule divergence.

  Game statistics (compute_game_statistics.py):
    - avg_branching_factor: mean legal moves per position (determines random
      baseline accuracy = 1/BF)
    - avg_game_length: mean moves per game
    - move_entropy: entropy of move frequency distribution (bits)
    - random_top1_acc: expected accuracy of random predictor

  Embedding-based divergence (TODO):
    Run variant games through pretrained OthelloGPT, collect residual stream
    activations at layer 4 or 5, compare distributions via MMD or Fréchet
    distance. Directly measures "how different do these games look to the model."


================================================================================
B. CAUSAL INTERVENTION EXPERIMENTS
================================================================================

Methodology (current, as of v5):
  - Intervene at resid_post of block N using layer N's NATIVE probe for both
    direction computation and local calibration
  - Per-cell binary search for minimal scale that flips probe at target layer
  - Calibration depth: 0 (local), 1 (+1 downstream), 2 (+2 downstream)
  - Measure probe accuracy at all downstream layers (each using its own probe)
  - Measure logit-level metrics: boundary margin, frac_positive, Li et al.
    top-N accuracy, legal probability mass
  - Per-move binary metrics: newly-legal enters top-N, above min-legal,
    newly-illegal dropped from top-N, below min-legal

Previous versions used layer 6 probe directions at layer 5 (cross-layer
mismatch). See abandoned_experiments.txt for v1-v3 details.

Per-move binary metrics (200 games with saved probs):
  Jobs: 5752339 (cd0), 5752340 (cd1), 5752337 (cd2), 5752341 (L4 cd2)

  Metric definitions:
    In Top-N:        newly-legal move appears in model's top-N predictions
                     (N = number of counterfactual-legal moves)
    Above Min-Legal: newly-legal move's prob >= weakest still-legal move's prob
    Dropped Top-N:   newly-illegal move falls out of model's top-N
    Below Min-Legal: newly-illegal move's prob drops below weakest still-legal

  Aggregate results (all conditions, N=1 flips/adds/mixed):

  Setting   | NL In Top-N | NL Above Min-Legal | NI Dropped Top-N | NI Below Min-Legal
  ----------|-------------|---------------------|------------------|-----------
  L5 cd0    |    51%      |         0%          |       31%        |     46%
  L5 cd1    |    69%      |         1%          |       64%        |     86%
  L5 cd2    |    82%      |         5%          |       84%        |     94%
  L4 cd2    |    81%      |         7%          |       79%        |     91%

  Fall-off with N (L5 cd2):
    Newly-legal  In Top-N:        82% (N=1) → 70% (N=2) → 69% (N=3)
    Newly-legal  Above Min-Legal:  5% (N=1) →  8% (N=2) → 12% (N=3)
    Newly-illegal Dropped Top-N:  84% (N=1) → 75% (N=2) → 69% (N=3)
    Newly-illegal Below Min-Legal: 94% (N=1) → 91% (N=2) → 89% (N=3)

  Key findings:
  1. Strong asymmetry: 94% of newly-illegal moves lose "legal" probability
     status, but only 5% of newly-legal moves gain it. Suppression >> promotion.
  2. "In Top-N" is misleadingly high because illegal moves have near-zero
     probability (~0.01%), so a tiny boost suffices to outrank them. "Above
     Min-Legal" is the honest test — newly-legal moves reach ~1-3% probability
     but legal moves sit at ~8-16%.
  3. For add conditions, 100% of newly-illegal moves are on the added cell
     (trivial: occupied square). For flip conditions, 0% are on modified cells
     (genuine flanking logic update).
  4. L4 cd2 ≈ L5 cd2: intervening one layer earlier gives similar results.
     The real lever is calibration depth, not intervention layer.
  5. Calibration depth is the dominant factor: cd0→cd2 at L5 is a much larger
     improvement than L5→L4 at cd2.

experiments/multi_intervention_probs{,_cd0,_cd1}/

B1c. Cascade intervention (Li et al. approach)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Intervene at every layer from start_layer to 7, each using that layer's
native probe for direction and local per-cell scale calibration. This follows
Li et al.'s approach of reinforcing the intervention at each layer rather than
relying on a single-layer intervention to propagate through the network.

  - N = 1, 2, 3, 8 simultaneous interventions
  - 5 conditions: flip_noninteracting, flip_interacting,
    add_noninteracting, add_interacting, mixed
  - Two runs: start at L4 (layers 4-7) and start at L5 (layers 5-7)
  - Saves full probability vectors (--save-probs)

Key findings:
  1. Cascade performs WORSE than single-layer cd2 on all per-move metrics,
     and is roughly comparable to single-layer cd0. The cascade applies
     locally-calibrated interventions at each layer, but the aggregate
     effect is much weaker than a single intervention calibrated to
     propagate 2 layers downstream.
  2. Likely explanation: each transformer block partially undoes the previous
     layer's intervention before the next cascade step is applied. The
     interventions don't stack constructively.
  3. NL Above Min-Legal remains at 0% — newly-legal moves never reach
     the probability floor of existing legal moves under cascade.
  4. Cascade does show slightly lower crosstalk (0.084 vs 0.107 for cd0),
     suggesting the per-layer local calibration is gentler but less effective.

How interventions propagate across layers. Intervene at one layer using native
probe, measure at all downstream layers. Sweeps calibration depth: 0 (local),
1 (+1 layer downstream), 2 (+2 layers downstream).

Three runs: layer 4, layer 5, layer 6. Each uses its own native probe.

A3. Spatially impossible board experiments (GER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cross-wire Othello flanking patterns to create rule sets that are structured
and compressible but cannot be embedded in any consistent 8x8 grid.

Method: For each pattern, with probability beta, swap its ray body (opponents +
terminal) with another pattern of the same direction and length whose target is
in the opposite board half (rows 0-3 vs 4-7). Swaps at the individual pattern
level create intra-cell contradictions: cell 16's right-length-1 pattern still
points to neighbor 17, but its right-length-2 pattern now points to cells from
row 5. No grid can satisfy both.

Spatial properties violated by cross-wiring:
  - Embeddability: no consistent 8x8 grid satisfies all constraints
  - Reflexivity: A→C flanks B, but C→A may not flank B
  - Transitivity: "right from A" → B, "right from B" → C, but C ≠ A+2
  - Collinearity: length-2 and length-3 patterns from same cell disagree on ray
  - Commutativity: right-then-down ≠ down-then-right
  - Distance symmetry: A references B at distance 2, B may never reference A
  - Neighborhood consistency: two directions from A may point to same cell
  - Betweenness: intermediate cells in long patterns absent from shorter patterns

Measured by Grid Embedding Residual (GER): extract all consecutive-pair
constraints from patterns with direction labels, solve least-squares for (x,y)
coordinates of each cell. GER = mean squared residual. Standard Othello → 0.

GER range achievable via cross-wiring: 0.0 to ~0.51. Compare: alpha corruption
has no spatial structure at all but is less compressible.

Beta sweep results (10 seeds each):

  0.0  | 0.000 ± 0.000  | 2381 ± 0        | 0.0

  0.2  | 0.171 ± 0.007  | 2525 ± 5        | 0.6

A8. Flanker Analysis (COMPLETE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each flanking rule with 3+ opponent pieces, compared the model's probability
for the target cell when:
  - "satisfied": all opponents present AND terminal has friendly piece
  - "unsatisfied": all opponents present AND terminal does NOT have friendly piece
  - "baseline": average probability for that cell at that move number

Results (100K games, 428 rules with length >= 3):

  Length  N_rules  Baseline  No_flank  With_flank  Chain_boost  Flank_boost  Chain%
  3      180      0.019     0.066     0.125       +0.047       +0.059       45%
  4      132      0.020     0.066     0.134       +0.046       +0.068       40%
  5       80      0.022     0.066     0.147       +0.044       +0.081       35%
  6       36      0.026     0.070     0.162       +0.045       +0.092       33%

Key findings:
  1. The model uses BOTH the opponent chain AND the terminal piece.
     100% of rules (all 428) show positive chain boost AND positive flank boost.

  2. The chain alone accounts for 30-45% of the total probability boost.
     The chain boost is roughly constant (~0.045) across all chain lengths.
     The flanker boost INCREASES with chain length (0.059 → 0.092).

  3. Without the flanker, the model still assigns 3.5x the baseline probability
     (0.066 vs 0.019). The opponent chain is a strong signal by itself.

  4. The flanker becomes MORE important for longer chains:
     Length 3: chain 45%, flanker 55%
     Length 6: chain 33%, flanker 67%

  5. Per-rule consistency: every single rule shows both effects. No rule has
     zero chain effect (min chain boost = 0.005). No rule has zero flanker
     effect (min flanker boost = 0.033).

  This revises the earlier "partial flanking" finding (from adversarial beam
  search, Section A4). The model DOES check for terminals — the earlier
  adversarial analysis showed that the model promotes cells when the chain
  is present without the terminal, but this new analysis shows the terminal
  adds substantial additional probability on top. The model uses a graded
  heuristic, not a binary check.

D4. Synthesis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three matched measurements all point to the same architectural advantage of
OGPT over the 1-layer MLP:

  Metric                          | MLP early | MLP late | OGPT early | OGPT late
  --------------------------------+-----------+----------+------------+----------
  Per-cell probe (center cells)   |   88.5%   |  69.9%   |  100.0%    |  97.9%
  Per-cell probe (inner cells)    |   99.0%   |  77.5%   |  100.0%    |  98.9%
  recall@K (output)               |   94.3%   |  87.5%   |  100.0%    |  99.7%

OGPT maintains uniform board encoding and output performance throughout the
game. The MLP loses internal representation steadily; OR-aggregation across
the 960 patterns hides some but not all of the loss in the output.

Interpretation: depth/attention is necessary for stable cross-turn board
encoding -- presumably because flip-tracking requires iterating
"current color = initial color XOR (number of flips mod 2)" which a single
Linear -> ReLU -> Linear cannot represent for cells with K > 2 flips.

Caveat: this establishes that depth gives OGPT a measurable representational
advantage, but does NOT establish that OGPT is doing "world modeling" in any
strong sense. It could be "more capacity to compute flip parity" without any
structural simulation. The intervention experiments (which work on the
1-layer MLP at 92% Li-top-K) show intervention success doesn't require
world modeling -- it requires linear decodability + downstream sensitivity
to that axis. Both architectures have those.

  - Turn specialists with move_grid (3600-d) input, same H=512
  - Compare to wheneven specialists at the same turn
  - If move_grid specialists outperform wheneven specialists -> richer input

  - If they don't outperform -> specialization captures most of what's
    available; richer input is redundant given a per-turn distribution

F3. Per-turn ENSEMBLE for unified inference
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If specialists outperform unified at their own turn, build a meta-MLP that
routes each test position to its turn-specialist. Aggregate top-1/recall@K
across the meta-model and compare to unified MLP. This gives the "what does
a panel of 10 specialized MLPs achieve" upper bound, informing whether the
OGPT-MLP gap is explainable by ensemble-style decomposition.

F4. Board decoding from the 960 PATTERN OUTPUTS (not hidden)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Train a linear probe on the 960 pattern logits (the output of the pattern
detector, not its hidden) -> 64x3 board state. Compare per-cell accuracy
to:
  - Hidden probe (~91% mean, 76% center)
  - 60-d cell-prob probe (lossy)
If output probe approaches hidden probe -> pattern outputs preserve most of
the board info despite the 1024 -> 960 projection. If much lower -> the
pattern detector's projection loses board-state structure.

F5. Pattern-level redundancy schemes (multi-instance per pattern)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Test whether output-level redundancy decorrelates pattern errors:
  - 5x output instances per pattern with sub-space splitting of hidden
  - Hidden-dropout-bagged training (each instance sees a random hidden mask)
If recall@K improves by >3pp -> output redundancy was a real lever and
scale up. If <1pp -> bottleneck is at the shared hidden, not the output.

E3. The by_black input degrades probe-readable board state
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Adding the forfeit-correct by_black channel (1.0 if cell c was originally
placed by black; precompute_by_black.py) actively hurts board decoding.
Probe accuracy drops from 91.25% baseline -> ~75% with by_black, and
center-cell accuracy drops from ~78% -> ~55%.

Per-region (uniform eval, H=512):

                                | corner | edge  | inner | center
  ------------------------------+--------+-------+-------+--------
  wheneven      (60-d baseline) |  100%  |  96%  |  88%  |  78%
  wheneven+by_black (180-d)     |  80.7% | 84.0% | 70.1% | 54.9%
  when+by_black     (120-d)     |  89.6% | 84.0% | 69.3% | 54.8%

Reading: by_black tells the model who PLACED each piece, which is correct
for cells that never flip (corners) but increasingly misleading for cells
that flip frequently (centers flip ~6x per game). The pattern detector
picks up by_black as a shortcut for "current color" and stops encoding the
full board structure in its hidden state. So the by_black-input variants
have HIGHER pat_acc (99.80% vs 99.71%) but LOWER probe-readable board
state.

Implication: "the MLP's hidden encodes board state at 91%" is partly
task-dependent. When the model has a non-board input shortcut, it uses it
and the hidden state degrades. This weakens the "MLP probe achieves 91% =
evidence of world model" interpretation -- the 91% is specifically
contingent on the model needing to compute the board internally.

E6. Cumulative rule-firings -> board state (REFUTED, jobs 5952575/5952644)

Hypothesis: reformat 60-cell move history as cumulative 960-d firing

Result: REFUTED.

  H=512, 3 epochs, 36 train chunks, eval=14.5M positions, both raw + mod-2

  Encoding | Board acc | corner | edge  | inner | center
  ---------+-----------+--------+-------+-------+--------
  raw      |  80.11%   |  92.0% | 86.2% | 75.4% |  69.0%
  mod-2    |  80.11%   |  92.0% | 86.2% | 75.4% |  69.0%

Raw and mod-2 are identical because EACH PATTERN FIRES 0 OR 1 TIMES per
game (a move can be played at most once, and a pattern with that target

The cumulative-firings input is WORSE than the 60-d move history (91.25%).
The 960-d binary vector tells you WHICH patterns fired in the game but
not WHEN — order is lost. Without order, the 1-layer MLP cannot recover
which flips applied after each placement. Confirms the bottleneck is
order-dependence, not depth alone.

================================================================================
E11. SYNTHESIS — STATE OF THE 1-LAYER VS OGPT COMPARISON (2026-05-14)
================================================================================

The 1-layer MLP shows the same intervention phenomenology as OGPT despite
having no internal recurrence or attention. Key data points from this
session:

  Metric                                      | MLP             | OGPT
  --------------------------------------------+-----------------+--------
  Probe board acc (uniform, H=512 wheneven)   |   91.25%        | ~99%
  Probe board acc center cells                |   75.8%         | ~99%
  Per-cell transition count diagnostic        |  Yes (E4)       |   -
  Random projection probe acc                 |   75-76%        |   -
  Intervention success (rank-based li_topk)   |   94.7% peak    |  82%
    (no calibration distortion)               |                 |
  Intervention boundary margin (cd0 K=3)      |   +2 to +5.6    |   -
  per-cell flip-count predictability          |   yes (E5)      |   -

The "world model" framing rests on probe accuracy + intervention success,
and both metrics distinguish neither qualitatively nor by a wide margin:
  - Probe gap (~5pp on mean, ~23pp on center cells) is consistent with
    "depth gives more XOR-of-flip-count capacity," not with a categorical
    "has world model vs doesn't."
  - Intervention success is HIGHER for the MLP than OGPT under the
    calibration-free rank metric.

The empirical width sweep (E10) shows the 1-layer ceiling is structural,
not capacity-limited. The per-cell pattern (E5) and the inverse correlation
to flip-count (E4) suggest a clean mechanistic story: depth is required
to compute the XOR-of-K-flips operation for cells with many transitions
per game.
